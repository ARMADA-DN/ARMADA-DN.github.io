# Voice

Who the site is for, and how it speaks to them. Read this before writing or
editing any copy. It covers the words the site shows its readers, not how the
repository's own documentation is written.

**Exception: `call.html`.** The 2025 call for candidates is a record of what was
published, and its copy is not edited, for voice or anything else. None of the
rules below apply to it. It still follows the visual design like every page:
markup and styles change with the rest of the site, its words do not.

## Audience

1. **Scholars**, first: researchers in data management, NLP and AI, and
   prospective doctoral candidates. They know the field, read for substance,
   and notice vague claims.
2. **Practitioners**: engineers and analysts in industry and the public sector
   who work with data and language models. They want to know what ARMADA does
   that matters in practice.
3. **Curious citizens** with a light technical background. They should be able
   to follow any page without already knowing what an LLM or a knowledge graph
   is.

Write for the scholar without losing the citizen: precise, but never assuming
jargon. Define a technical term on first use, or say what it does instead.

## Tone

**Institutional but approachable.** The program is highly prestigious and
selective; the team behind it is young, dynamic and working at the edge of the
technology; and it is down to earth, aiming at real impact rather than at
sounding impressive.

- **Confident, not boastful.** State what ARMADA does and let the facts carry
  the weight: 15 doctoral candidates, 8 organizations, 7 countries. No
  "world-class", "cutting-edge" or "revolutionary".
- **Concrete over abstract.** Name the problem, the people and the outcome. "We
  check that an answer can be traced back to the data" beats "we ensure
  trustworthy AI".
- **Energetic, not breathless.** Short sentences, active verbs, the present
  tense where it is true. No exclamation marks.
- **Plain about impact.** Say who benefits and how: medicine, education,
  business intelligence, EU policy on AI.

### Who speaks

- **"ARMADA"** in institutional blocks: what the network is, its aims, its
  funding, its structure. "ARMADA brings together 8 organizations from 7
  countries."
- **"We"** where real people are involved: events, people, results, news. "We
  welcomed our 15 doctoral candidates in Verona." "We present the vision at
  the core of the project."
- Address the reader as "you" only where they are asked to act: apply, get in
  touch, follow.

## Language

- **English**, on every page (`lang="en"`). No page is translated.
- **American spelling**: program, specialize, organization, behavior.
- Dates as "January 12–17, 2026"; places as "Verona, Italy".
- Numbers as figures from 10 up, and always for counts that matter (15
  doctoral candidates, 8 organizations, 5 work packages).

## Terminology

- **ARMADA**: always in capitals. The full name, on first mention on a page
  where it helps: "Reliable Conversational Domain-specific Data Exploration and
  Analysis".
- **The network**: "the ARMADA Doctoral Network", or "MSCA Doctoral Network"
  where the funding scheme matters. Not "the consortium" in reader-facing copy.
- **Doctoral candidates**: the researchers ARMADA trains. "DCs" only after the
  full term on the same page. Not "Early Stage Researchers", which is the
  retired MSCA term, nor "PhD students".
- **Work packages**: "WP1: Efficiency", "WP2: Grounding", "WP3:
  Explainability", "WP4: Soundness", "WP5: Guidance". Tasks as "T1.1".
- **Partners** by their full name on first mention, then their short name:
  "Université Paris Cité (UPC)".
- **Technical terms**: "large language models (LLMs)" and "conversational data
  analytics (CDA)" spelled out on first use on a page, lowercase unless they
  start a sentence or heading.
- **Funding**: "Horizon Europe, Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions (MSCA) Doctoral
  Networks, Grant Agreement No. 101168951". The footer's wording is required by
  the grant and is not edited for style.
