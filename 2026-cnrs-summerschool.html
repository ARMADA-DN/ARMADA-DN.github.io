Summer School 2026

April 26 - May 2, 2026 | Cargèse, Corsica, France

The ARMADA Summer School brings together the 15 doctoral candidates and a selection of external participants from the broader database research community for an intensive week of training, research presentations, and networking at the Institute for Scientific Studies of Cargèse, University of Corsica.

For questions or comments, please contact the project coordinator: Matteo Lissandrini at project.armada@ateneo.univr.it

About the Event

The ARMADA Summer School 2026 is a key training event of the MSCA Doctoral Networks program, held at the scenic Institute for Scientific Studies of Cargèse in Corsica. The school combines lectures, hands-on practical exercises, a poster session, student presentations, and social activities — offering doctoral candidates a rich environment to deepen their expertise and strengthen collaboration across all partner institutions.

Main Topics Covered

  • Research methodology and scientific experimentation
  • Data reduction and data provenance
  • Data modalities and explainable AI
  • Reasoning with Large Language Models
  • AI for Code: from language models to coding agents
  • AI regulation
  • Green AI and sustainable computing
  • High-dimensional vector indexing and similarity search

Speakers and Instructors

Prof. Nicolas Travers ESILV, Paris

Scientific Experimentation Methodology

Introduces key methodologies for conducting scientific experiments in computer science research, covering empirical studies, feasibility studies, comparative studies, literature surveys, and simulations — illustrated through examples in distributed databases, NoSQL systems, and data analytics.

Prof. Yannis Velegrakis Utrecht University, Netherlands

Data Reduction

Lecture and hands-on practical exercises covering foundational and advanced techniques for data reduction in large-scale data management scenarios.

Prof. Katja Hose, Prof. Silviu Maniu & Dr. Maxime Jakubowski TU Wien / Université Grenoble Alpes

Data Provenance

A joint lecture exploring the principles and practices of data provenance — how to track, represent, and reason about the origins and transformations of data in complex pipelines.

Prof. Jasmina Bogojeska ZHAW School of Engineering, Switzerland

Data Modalities and Explainable AI

Examines heterogeneous data modalities and explainability techniques in AI systems, with practical exercises bridging theory and real-world deployment challenges.

Prof. Akhil Arora Aarhus University, Denmark

Reasoning with Large Language Models

Traces the landscape of LLM reasoning from chain-of-thought prompting and test-time scaling to Fleet of Agents (FoA) and CacheSaver — covering how to select inference-time strategies, evaluate them rigorously, and deploy them sustainably.

Dr. Behrooz Omidvar-Tehrani AWS Amazon, USA

AI for Code: From Language Models to Coding Agents

Covers the evolution from code-generating language models to fully agentic coding systems, with practical exercises exploring state-of-the-art tools and real-world software engineering use cases.

Prof. Robin Plique Université Paris 1 Sorbonne, France

AI Regulation

Surveys the evolving legal and regulatory landscape for AI in Europe and beyond, examining frameworks for accountability, transparency, and compliance in AI-driven systems.

Dr. Nicolas Burger Optimistik, France

Green AI

Addresses the environmental footprint of AI systems, presenting techniques for measuring and reducing energy consumption while maintaining model performance — with a focus on optimization and system safety.

Prof. Themis Palpanas Université Paris Cité, France

High-Dimensional Vector Indexing and Similarity Search

Presents algorithmic and systems advances in indexing and querying high-dimensional vector spaces — a foundational capability for modern AI and data management applications.
